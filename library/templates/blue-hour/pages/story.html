of blue, on a flat sea, with the lighthouse still lit. A long evening told in the order it happened.

Moon, sea and the Hamnøy light, 21:40. 25 s at f/4, ISO 800.

At 21:12 the sun had been gone for an hour and the sea had gone the colour of a bruise. Nobody spoke. Eight tripods stood in a loose line on the rocks, and the only sound was the shutter of someone who could not wait.

The trick with blue hour is that it is not an hour. On this coast, in January, it is closer to eleven minutes: the sky has to be dark enough that the lighthouse beam reads, and bright enough that the water still holds a colour. Miss either end and you have a nice picture of nothing.

What we got wrong, twice

The first night we arrived late and shot into the sun. The second we arrived early and shot the wrong rock. On the third we sat on the beach for forty minutes with our backs to the view, and turned around together at 21:29.

Patience is a lens, and it is the only one you cannot lose.

Here is what I tell every group on the walk down: pick one foreground, one frame and one setting. When the colour arrives, do not change any of them. Change your breathing instead.

Same evening, 23:05, looking the other way. 10 s at f/2.8, ISO 3200.
