Why we keep doing what we regret

By the Mind Notes teamReviewed by a registered psychologist (replace)

You promised yourself you wouldn't. And yet, there you are, scrolling at midnight, again. It is easy to read this as a flaw in character. Psychology offers a gentler and more useful reading.

Habits are not decisions, they are shortcuts. The brain stores a cue, a routine and a reward, and then runs them with very little attention. Regret arrives afterwards, when attention has caught up.

Willpower is a poor plan. A better one is to change the cue.

Cue, routine, reward

Look at the cue first: the time, place, feeling or person that usually comes before the habit. Change one small thing about it, and the routine often loosens without a fight.

Make the better thing easy

Put the book on the pillow. Move the phone across the room. These are not tricks; they are how attention actually works.
