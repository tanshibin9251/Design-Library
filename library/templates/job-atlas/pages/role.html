Credit Analyst 信贷分析员

Decides whether the bank lends the money — by working out how likely it is to come back.

AnalystOffice-basedDegree needed

01A day in the life

  1. 19:00 amOpen a new loan application and pull the applicant’s repayment history from the central credit register.
  2. 210:30 amCross-check the credit bureau score to rate overall risk.
  3. 312:00 pmRead three years of company accounts and work out whether profits can cover the repayments.
  4. 414:00 pmWrite the credit report: approve, decline, or approve with a smaller limit — and say why.
  5. 516:00 pmSpot a number that doesn’t add up, then call the applicant or the relationship manager to clear it.

02What you actually do

  • Review loan and credit applications and the applicant’s financial statements.
  • Judge repayment ability and the chance of default.
  • Write the credit report and recommend approve, decline or adjust.
  • Watch existing borrowers for early warning signs.
  • Agree loan conditions with relationship managers and the risk team.

04What it pays

Monthly salary, in ringgit. The ladder is a range — where you land depends on the three things below.

Bank typeCorporate and commercial lending pays more than personal loans — the cases are harder and the stakes higher.
LocationMost head offices and approval centres sit in the Klang Valley, and pay follows.
CertificatesChartered Banker or a credit-professional certificate is the usual ticket to manager level.

05Getting in

  • Education: a degree in economics, business, finance or accounting.
  • Skills: reading financial statements; a feel for credit risk.
  • Interview: expect a short case — a simplified set of accounts, and you give a yes or no on the spot. Most banks train new hires on their own systems.

07How it earns its keep

A bank’s biggest risk is lending money that never comes back. Every careful decision here protects the loan book — and a sharp analyst lets the bank lend more, safely.

08Good and not so good

The good

  • Analysis skills transfer to almost any finance job.
  • Pay climbs clearly with seniority.
  • Little pressure to hit sales targets.

The less good

  • A wrong call lands on the bank, and on you.
  • You must keep up with the economy and each industry.
  • Busy periods mean tight turnaround times.

09A common myth

The myth

“It’s just ticking boxes and letting loans through.”

The reality

Numbers are only half of it. You weigh industry trends, how trustworthy management is, and the wider economy — judgement that grows with experience, not a formula.
